A luxury home and lifestyle retailer was investing heavily in paid advertising. Visitors were landing on its product pages, but too few were becoming customers.

I owned the work from diagnosis to delivery. After analysing visitor behaviour, I independently developed and implemented the test variant in the Shopify theme, then managed the A/B test against the original.

01 — The commercial challengeATTENTION HAD A COST.

Paying for the visit.
Losing the
opportunity.

More traffic only helps when visitors can move forward.

The client expected advertising to generate sales. Instead, the product page placed important buying information beyond the area many visitors were reaching.

That made the page itself a priority: improve the experience of the people already arriving.

02 — The hypothesis
Bring the information closer.
Make the next step easier.
IF → THEN

If I reduced the gallery’s vertical footprint and brought product details, pricing, variants and the main Add to Cart control closer to the initial viewport, more visitors could evaluate the product and act before leaving.

What I changed

Strategy, built
and tested by me.

  1. Reduce unused vertical space

    Condense the product gallery area so visitors reach essential information sooner.

  2. Restructure the buying experience

    Bring product information, pricing, options and the main purchase action closer together.

  3. Implement and test

    I independently coded and implemented the variant in the Shopify theme, then managed the experiment against the original layout.

ACTUAL EXPERIMENT OUTPUT

More visitors converted.
More revenue per session, on average.

Variation B recorded 178 orders versus 124 for control. With slightly more traffic assigned to B, revenue per visitor provides useful context: it rose from Rs122.995 to Rs159.448, alongside the improvement in conversion rate.

How to read these results +

Relative, not percentage-point lifts. The reported +34.25% conversion lift is consistent with the underlying order/session rates. The displayed rounded rates differ by 0.83 percentage points. Revenue and RPV lifts are relative changes too.

Traffic and revenue. B received 5,464 sessions versus 5,110 for A. The +38.62% figure compares total test revenue; it is not a traffic-normalised revenue lift or a measured improvement in advertising ROAS. The dashboard’s RPV values correspond to revenue divided by sessions.

Confidence and trend views. The project reported a winning variant at 95% confidence; the supplied metrics capture does not display that calculation. The conversion trend’s visual endpoints differ from the table’s final rates, so the table governs numerical claims. Graphs are preserved as supplied, including their original labels.

Scope of the conclusion. These are aggregate experiment results, not proof of the same lift on every device or product. The test supports the winning page experience; it does not isolate each behavioural mechanism.

05 — Why the change makes senseBEHAVIOURAL INTERPRETATION

Make the decision
easier to reach.

The experiment shows a stronger outcome for the optimised experience. These are plausible explanations for that outcome, rather than separately proven psychological effects.

01 / DISCOVERY

Less effort to find
what matters.

A shorter route to price and product details could reduce the effort needed to decide whether the item is relevant.

02 / EVALUATION

More context for
the next decision.

Seeing the product, price and available options closer together may help visitors evaluate the offer with less back-and-forth scrolling.

03 / ACTION

A clearer path
from intent to cart.

Placing the main purchase control near the decision information makes the next step easier to access when a visitor is ready.
